## 1. Canada — Federal Layer (FINTRAC MSB/FMSB)

| Function | Regulator | Licence/Registration | Filing Requirements | Timeline |
|----------|-----------|---------------------|---------------------|----------|
| **Payments** | FINTRAC | MSB Registration (if payment processing involves fund transmission) | Business details, ownership, compliance officer, AML program | 3–6 weeks FINTRAC processing; 8–16 weeks realistic end-to-end |
| **E-money** | FINTRAC | MSB Registration (if issuing/redeeming negotiable instruments or transmitting funds) | Same as above; no separate e-money licence | Same |
| **FX** | FINTRAC | MSB Registration (foreign exchange dealing) | Same; FX is a named MSB activity category | Same |
| **Remittance** | FINTRAC | MSB Registration (money transfer/remittance) | Same; must register **before** commencing operations | Same |
| **Card Issuing** | FINTRAC | MSB Registration (if issuing payment cards tied to fund transmission) | No dedicated card-issuing licence at federal level; falls under MSB if transmitting funds | Same |
| **Stored Value** | FINTRAC | MSB Registration (if issuing/redeeming stored-value instruments) | Same; stored-value may trigger MSB activity | Same |

### Key Federal Requirements (all functions)

- **No government fee** for FINTRAC MSB registration
- **No minimum capital** at federal level
- **Physical office required** — virtual offices and P.O. boxes rejected
- **Compliance officer** must be appointed; must have genuine authority
- **Written AML/CTF program** required **before** registration, not after
- **Registration valid 2 years**; renewal mandatory
- **FMSB** (Foreign MSB): for businesses with no Canadian physical presence but serving Canadian clients — same AML obligations, no provincial licensing

> **Foreign MSB (FMSB) Note:** No physical Canadian office required; still must register with FINTRAC and maintain AML program. Cannot obtain provincial licences (which require local presence).

### Ontario

| Function | Regulator | Licence | Notes |
|----------|-----------|---------|-------|
| All money services | **FSRA** (Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario) | Provincial MSB registration required | Fees CAD $500–$2,000; surety bond CAD $50,000–$100,000 |
| Timeline | — | — | Provincial processing adds several weeks to federal timeline |

### Quebec

| Function | Regulator | Licence | Notes |
|----------|-----------|---------|-------|
| All money services | **AMF** (Autorité des marchés financiers) | Provincial MSB licence | **Required if serving Quebec residents or have Quebec establishment** |
| Timeline | — | — | **6–9 months** — significantly slower than FINTRAC |
| Requirements | — | — | Security clearance for directors, officers, 10%+ shareholders; surety bond CAD $25,000–$500,000 based on volume |

### British Columbia

| Function | Regulator | Licence | Notes |
|----------|-----------|---------|-------|
| All money services | **BCFSA** (BC Financial Services Authority) | Provincial MSB registration (Bill 19–2023) | Gatekeeping legislation; focused on preventing bad actors at entry |
| Timeline | — | — | Registration details still developing; surety bond CAD $10,000 minimum |
| Exemptions | — | — | Insurance companies, banks, trust companies, securities dealers, casinos exempt |

> **Critical Note:** Quebec is the most restrictive provincial regime. If you serve Quebec residents, budget **6–9 additional months** and significant compliance costs. BC follows Quebec's gatekeeping model but is still implementing rules. Ontario requires provincial registration alongside federal.

## 3. Mexico — CNBV / Fintech Law (Ley Fintech 2018)

| Function | Regulator | Licence | Key Requirements | Timeline |
|----------|-----------|---------|------------------|----------|
| **Payments** | **CNBV** + Banco de México + SHCP (tripartite) | **IFPE** (Institución de Fondos de Pago Electrónico) | Minimum capital: 500,000–700,000 UDIs (~MXN 4–5.5M) | Not specified in sources; expect 12–24 months for authorization |
| **E-money** | CNBV | IFPE | Electronic money issuance, digital wallets, fund transfers | Same |
| **FX** | CNBV/Banco de México | IFPE + specific central bank authorization for foreign currency | Additional authorization required for FX operations | Same |
| **Remittance** | CNBV | IFPE | Fund transfers between users/merchants | Same |
| **Card Issuing** | CNBV | IFPE (as payment instrument issuer) | Card issuance tied to e-money/payment accounts | Same |
| **Stored Value** | CNBV | IFPE | Digital wallet/stored-value accounts | Same |

### IFPE Licence Scope

- Issue, manage, and redeem **electronic money**
- Manage **digital wallets**
- Facilitate **fund transfers** between users or merchants
- Process payments in **MXN or digital assets** (with central bank authorization)

### Key Requirements

- **Local presence**: Physical office in Mexico + local legal representative
- **Corporate governance**: Board structure, shareholder disclosure, clean records for executives
- **AML/KYC**: Automated transaction monitoring; reporting to Financial Intelligence Unit (UIF)
- **SPEI connectivity**: Strategic advantage for real-time payments
- **No IFPE licence** = cannot legally issue, manage, or redeem e-money in Mexico

> **Strategic Note:** Mexico's Fintech Law is restrictive — only IFPE licensees can offer regulated payment services. **No sandbox or lighter-touch regime** for payments. The tripartite approval process (CNBV + Banco de México + SHCP) adds complexity and time.

## 4. Brazil — Banco Central do Brasil (BCB)

| Function | Regulator | Licence | Key Requirements | Timeline |
|----------|-----------|---------|------------------|----------|
| **Payments** | **BCB** | **Instituição de Pagamento (IP)** — four modalities | Capital: **R$ 9.2M–R$ 32.8M** (risk-based) | **6–18 months** |
| **E-money** | BCB | IP — **Emissor de Moeda Eletrônica** | Included in IP licence; modality must be specified in application | Same |
| **FX** | BCB | IP + FX authorization | FX services require separate BCB authorization; PSAVs now integrated into FX market | Same |
| **Remittance** | BCB | IP — fund transfer | Included in payment services; no separate remittance licence | Same |
| **Card Issuing** | BCB | IP — **Emissor de Instrumento de Pagamento Pós-pago** | Post-paid instrument issuance (credit/prepaid cards) | Same |
| **Stored Value** | BCB | IP — Emissor de Moeda Eletrônica | Electronic money accounts | Same |

### IP Modalities (must specify in application)

1. **Emissor de Moeda Eletrônica** — e-money issuance
2. **Emissor de Instrumento de Pagamento Pós-pago** — post-paid instruments (cards)
3. **Credenciador** — merchant acquiring
4. **Iniciador de Transação de Pagamento (ITP)** — payment initiation

### Critical 2025–2026 Changes

- **Pre-authorization mandatory** since September 2025 (Resoluções BCB 494/495) — **no more operating first, getting authorized later**
- **Capital requirements increased dramatically**: from R$1M–R$9M to **R$9.2M–R$32.8M** depending on risk/complexity
- **Physical office required** — coworking and virtual offices prohibited
- **Transition period**: Capital phased in 25% (Dec 2026), 50% (Jun 2027), 75% (Dec 2027), 100% (Jan 2028)

### Virtual Asset Service Providers (PSAVs) — New Regime

- **Resoluções BCB 519, 520, 521** (Nov 2025) regulate crypto/virtual assets under Law 14.478/2022
- **Authorization required** from BCB; existing PSAVs must apply within 270 days of Feb 2, 2026
- **Segregation of client assets** mandatory; own crypto limited to 5% of client holdings for liquidity
- **FX integration**: Cross-border crypto transfers now classified as FX operations; reporting to BCB by 5th of following month

> **Strategic Note:** Brazil has **tightened dramatically** in 2025–2026. The era of launching without authorization is over. Capital requirements are now substantial, and physical presence is mandatory. However, **Banking as a Service (BaaS)** under Resolução Conjunta 16/2025 allows non-licensed companies to offer payment accounts and credit via a licensed IP partner, reducing time-to-market and capital burden.

## 5. United States — Federal + State Overview

> **Note:** US state money transmitter licensing is the primary regime for payments, e-money, FX, remittance, and stored value. Federal registration (FinCEN MSB) is required but separate from state licensing.

| Function | Primary Regulator | Licence | Key Facts |
|----------|------------------|---------|-----------|
| **Payments** | State banking departments (NYDFS, etc.) + FinCEN | State Money Transmitter Licence (MTL) + FinCEN MSB registration | **Nationwide coverage requires 49+ state licences** (Montana is the only state without MTL requirement) |
| **E-money** | State regulators | MTL + possibly specialized e-money licence (varies by state) | No federal e-money charter; state-by-state |
| **FX** | State regulators + FinCEN | MTL (if transmitting funds) + FinCEN MSB | FX dealer may require separate state registration |
| **Remittance** | State regulators + FinCEN | MTL + FinCEN MSB | Core MTL activity |
| **Card Issuing** | Federal (OCC/FDIC/Fed) if bank; state if non-bank | Bank charter or state MTL + FinCEN | Non-bank card issuers face state-by-state regulation |
| **Stored Value** | State regulators | MTL + possible money transmission/stored value licence | Some states have specific stored-value rules |

### Key US Realities

- **No single US payments licence** — state-by-state patchwork
- **FinCEN MSB registration** is federal but does not authorize operations; state licences are operational gate
- **Surety bonds** required in most states (typically $50K–$500K+)
- **Timeline**: 12–24 months for nationwide coverage; individual state licences 2–6 months each
- **Cost**: $100K–$500K+ for nationwide licensing (legal, bonds, fees)

> **Not covered in detail** due to search results limitation for US-specific state licensing requirements.

## 6. Colombia — Preliminary Notes (Incomplete)

Colombia's regime is **less documented** in available sources. Key findings:

- **Lead regulator**: Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC)
- **Core law**: Law 2222 of 2022 (Fintech)
- **Remittance/FX**: Must operate through **Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario (IMC)** — includes banks, financial corporations, brokerage firms, and **Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos**
- **Approval timeline**: 6–12 months for SFC authorization
- **Entry capital**: COP 500M (Financial Group)

## 8. Key Decision Points

### For Canada Entry

1. **Start with FINTRAC MSB registration** — no fee, no capital minimum
2. **Add Quebec AMF only if serving Quebec residents** — adds 6–9 months and significant compliance burden
3. **Ontario FSRA and BC BCFSA** are secondary layers for those provinces
4. **Physical Canadian office required** — no virtual offices

### For Mexico Entry

1. **IFPE licence is the only path** for regulated payments/e-money
2. **Tripartite approval** (CNBV + Banco de México + SHCP) adds complexity
3. **Local physical presence and legal representative required**
4. **SPEI connectivity** is the key strategic benefit once licensed

### For Brazil Entry

1. **Pre-authorization is now mandatory** — no launch-first approach
2. **Capital requirements are substantial** (R$9.2M+)
3. **Consider BaaS model** (Resolução Conjunta 16/2025) to launch via licensed partner without own IP licence
4. **Crypto/PSAV regime is separate** and additional to IP

### For US Entry

1. **Budget 12–24 months and $100K–$500K+** for nationwide licensing
2. **FinCEN MSB registration is table stakes** — not operational authorization
3. **State-by-state MTLs** are the real barrier
