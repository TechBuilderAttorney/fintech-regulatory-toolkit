### TILA / Regulation Z – Explanatory Note

**Purpose:** Regulation Z implements TILA. TILA is the statute; Reg Z is the CFPB's rule that carries it into effect.

**Rule:** Covered creditors must provide clear, upfront disclosure of key credit terms **before** a consumer becomes obligated on a consumer credit transaction. Applies to any creditor that regularly extends consumer credit.

**Core requirements:**
- **APR disclosure** – The cost of credit expressed as a yearly rate.
- **Finance charge** – The dollar amount the credit will cost the consumer.
- **Amount financed** – The principal amount of credit provided.
- **Total of payments** – The total the consumer will pay over the life of the credit.
- **Payment schedule** – The number, amount, and timing of payments.
- **Timing** – Before consummation (closed-end); at account opening and via periodic statements (open-end).
- **Coverage** – Consumer credit: mortgages, HELOCs, credit cards, installment loans, certain student loans.
- **Exemption threshold** – Higher-priced mortgage loan threshold for 2026 is **$73,400** (adjusts annually).
- **EWA (Earned Wage Access)** – May not be "credit" under TILA — evolving area.
- **Reg Z subparts** – Subpart B (open-end credit), Subpart C (closed-end credit), Subpart E (mortgages), Subpart F (reverse mortgages).
- **Right of rescission** – For certain home-secured loans, the consumer may cancel within **3 business days**.
- **Advertising rules** – Triggering terms (e.g., "no down payment," specific APR) require additional disclosures.
- **Common pitfalls** – Late fees, origination fees, misleading advertising, failure to disclose before commitment.

**Source:** CFPB, 12 C.F.R. Part 1026 (Regulation Z), implementing the Truth in Lending Act.

**Key takeaways:**
- TILA/Reg Z = disclosure of credit terms, not a prohibition on conduct.
- Applies to consumer credit only.
- Disclosures must be clear, conspicuous, and delivered before the consumer commits.
- Reg Z subparts govern different credit types.
- UDAAP is the separate conduct standard. TILA is the disclosure standard. Both apply to consumer credit products.
