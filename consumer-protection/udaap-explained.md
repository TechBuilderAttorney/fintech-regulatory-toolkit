# UDAAP – Explanatory Note

## Purpose of UDAAP

UDAAP (Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices) is the CFPB's catch-all consumer protection standard under Dodd-Frank Act §§ 1031 and 1036. Its purpose is to:

- **Prohibit unfair, deceptive, and abusive conduct** by any provider of consumer financial products or services.
- **Fill gaps** left by specific disclosure statutes like TILA — UDAAP reaches conduct, not just disclosures.
- **Protect consumers** from harm they cannot reasonably avoid, from misleading representations, and from exploitation of unequal bargaining power.
- **Extend to service providers** — not just the financial institution itself.
- **Authorize the CFPB** to supervise, examine, and enforce against covered entities.

## Who It Covers

Any provider of consumer financial products or services, and their service providers. This includes banks, nonbanks, fintechs, payments companies, and their vendors.

## The Three Prohibitions

### 1. Unfair — Three-Part Test (all three must be met)

- **Substantial injury** — Monetary harm, or small harm to many consumers. Risk of concrete harm counts. Trivial or speculative harms do not.
- **Not reasonably avoidable** — Consumers cannot escape the harm. Hidden terms, coercion, or withholding material information until after commitment.
- **Not outweighed by countervailing benefits** — The harm is not justified by benefits to consumers or competition.

### 2. Deceptive — Three-Part Test

- **Likely to mislead** — Look at the overall net impression, not isolated statements.
- **Reasonable consumer interpretation** — Judged from the target audience's perspective.
- **Material** — Likely to affect the consumer's choice. Costs, benefits, and restrictions are presumed material.

**FTC "four Ps" test:** Prominence, Presentation, Placement, Proximity.

### 3. Abusive — Two Prongs

- **Materially interferes** with the consumer's ability to understand a term or condition; **or**
- **Takes unreasonable advantage** of the consumer's lack of understanding, inability to protect their interests, or reliance on the entity.

## Penalties

- **Ordinary:** up to $5,000 per day
- **Reckless:** up to $25,000 per day
- **Knowing:** up to $1,000,000 per day

## Discrimination as UDAAP

The CFPB's updated Exam Manual (March 2022) explicitly incorporates anti-discrimination considerations. Discrimination may meet the criteria for unfairness by causing substantial harm consumers cannot reasonably avoid.

## CFPB Supervisory Authority

The CFPB examines banks and nonbanks, including service providers, for UDAAP compliance. Examinations cover marketing, fees, disclosures, and UI design.

## Private Right of Action

No private UDAAP claim under Dodd-Frank, but states may have parallel laws (UDAP).

## Enforcement Examples

Wells Fargo, Navient, Betterment — enforcement actions demonstrating the CFPB's reach across marketing, servicing, and fees.

## Key Takeaways

- UDAAP = conduct standard, not a disclosure standard.
- Unfair = substantial injury + not reasonably avoidable + not outweighed by benefits.
- Deceptive = likely to mislead + reasonable interpretation + material.
- Abusive = material interference OR unreasonable advantage.
- Penalties up to $1M per day for knowing violations.
- Discrimination may be treated as unfair.
- Applies to marketing, fees, UI design, and service providers.
- UDAAP and TILA work together: TILA requires disclosure, UDAAP prohibits harmful conduct.
