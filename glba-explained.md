# GLBA Explained: Coverage, Privacy, and Safeguards

## The Threshold Question: "Significantly Engaged"

Before asking what GLBA requires, you must ask whether GLBA applies at all. A business is a "financial institution" under GLBA if it is "significantly engaged" in financial activities under Section 4(k) of the Bank Holding Company Act. This is a facts-and-circumstances test, not a bright-line rule.

The FTC weighs two factors:

**1. Formal arrangement.** Is the financial activity provided through a formal, ongoing arrangement or product?

- Covered: issuing a credit card, wire transfer services, money transmission, lending.
- Not covered: running a tab, occasional lay-away.

**2. Frequency.** How regularly does the business engage in the financial activity?

- Covered: regularly wiring money, ongoing payment services.
- Not covered: isolated or occasional financial activity.

The stronger and more frequent the activity, the more likely the business is covered.

**Second form of coverage:** Even if a business is not itself a financial institution, it can still be subject to GLBA's reuse and redisclosure limits if it receives nonpublic personal information (NPI) from a nonaffiliated financial institution.

**Why this matters for fintech:** Payments, money transmission, FX, remittance, stored value, and card issuing likely trigger GLBA coverage.

## Part One: The Privacy Rule

**Opt-out matrix.** Sharing NPI with nonaffiliated third parties outside the statutory exceptions requires an opt-out. Section 313.13 permits sharing with service providers and for joint marketing with notice but no opt-out. Sections 313.14 and 313.15 permit sharing without opt-out and with a simplified customer notice.

**Three exceptions:**

- Section 313.13 — service providers and joint marketing (requires a written confidentiality contract).
- Section 313.14 — processing and administering a requested transaction.
- Section 313.15 — fraud prevention, legal compliance, subpoenas, auditors, loan purchasers.

**Reuse and redisclosure limits.** If NPI is received under Section 313.14 or 313.15, it may only be used to carry out the purpose for which it was received. Outside those exceptions, the recipient may use the information internally but may only redisclose it consistent with the originating institution's privacy policy.

## Part Two: The Safeguards Rule

**Source:** FTC Safeguards Rule, 16 C.F.R. Part 314 (updated May 2024).

**Rule:** Covered financial institutions must develop, implement, and maintain a comprehensive written information security program (WISP). The same "significantly engaged" coverage test applies.

**Core requirements:**

- **WISP** — Written program appropriate to the size and complexity of the business.
- **Administrative, technical, and physical safeguards** — Designated employee(s) to oversee; risk assessment; employee training; service provider oversight.
- **Multi-factor authentication (MFA)** — Required for any information system accessed by employees.
- **Annual board report** — At least annually, report on overall security program status and compliance.
- **Service provider oversight** — Contractual requirements and periodic assessments.
- **Breach notification** — Notify the FTC within 30 days of discovering a security event affecting 500+ consumers.

## Key Distinction

| Rule | What It Governs |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Privacy Rule** | Notice, opt-out, and limits on sharing NPI |
| **Safeguards Rule** | Security of NPI — WISP, MFA, board reporting, breach notice |

Both apply to financial institutions. A fintech that triggers GLBA coverage must comply with both.
