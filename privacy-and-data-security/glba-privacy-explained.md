### GLBA Privacy Rule – Coverage Test: "Significantly Engaged"

**Source:** FTC, How to Comply with the Privacy of Consumer Financial Information Rule of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act.

**Rule:** A business is a "financial institution" under GLBA if it is "significantly engaged" in financial activities under BHC Act §4(k). This is a facts-and-circumstances test.

**Two factors:**
1. **Formal arrangement** – Is the financial activity provided through a formal, ongoing arrangement or product?
   - Covered: issuing a credit card, wire transfer service, money transmission, lending.
   - Not covered: running a tab, occasional lay-away.
2. **Frequency** – How regularly does the business engage in the financial activity?
   - Covered: regularly wiring money, ongoing payments services.
   - Not covered: isolated or occasional financial activity.

**Application:** Stronger/more frequent activity = likely covered. FTC weighs both factors; no bright-line.

**Second form of coverage:** Even if not a financial institution, you are subject to reuse/redisclosure limits if you receive NPI from a nonaffiliated financial institution.

**Related quick entries:**
- **Opt-out matrix:** Nonaffiliated third-party sharing outside exceptions = opt-out required. §13 = notice, no opt-out. §14/§15 = no opt-out, simplified customer notice.
- **Three exceptions:** §313.13 (service providers/joint marketing, written confidentiality contract); §313.14 (processing/administering requested transaction); §313.15 (fraud, legal compliance, subpoenas, auditors, loan purchasers).
- **Reuse/redisclosure limits:** If received under §14/§15, use only to carry out purpose received. Outside §14/§15, may use internally but redisclose only consistent with originating institution’s privacy policy.
