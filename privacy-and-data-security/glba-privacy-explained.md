### GLBA Privacy Rule – Coverage Test: "Significantly Engaged"

## Purpose of the GLBA Privacy Rule

The GLBA Privacy Rule (16 C.F.R. Part 313) implements the privacy provisions of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act. Its purpose is to:

- **Protect consumer financial privacy** by limiting when a financial institution may disclose a consumer's nonpublic personal information (NPI) to nonaffiliated third parties.
- **Require transparency** by mandating that financial institutions notify customers about their information-sharing practices.
- **Give consumers control** by providing a right to "opt out" of certain information sharing with nonaffiliated third parties.
- **Restrict downstream use** by limiting how recipients of NPI may reuse and redisclose that information.
- **Prohibit specific conduct** by banning the sharing of account numbers for marketing purposes, even with consent.

## Key Definitions

- **Consumer** — Someone who obtains a financial product or service for personal, family, or household purposes. Business/commercial clients are generally excluded.
- **Customer** — A consumer with a continuing relationship with the financial institution. Former customers are treated as consumers.
- **NPI (Nonpublic Personal Information)** — Personally identifiable financial information collected in connection with providing a financial product or service. Includes application data, transaction data, account numbers, balances, payment history, and third-party data. Publicly available information is not NPI unless the individual can and has not opted out. Lists derived partly from NPI remain NPI.

## Core Requirements

- **Privacy notices** — Customers get an initial notice by the time the relationship is established, plus an annual notice while the relationship lasts. Non-customer consumers get notice only before sharing NPI with nonaffiliated third parties outside exceptions; short-form notice allowed.
- **Opt-out notices** — Required for sharing NPI with nonaffiliated third parties outside exceptions. Must provide a reasonable means to opt out (toll-free number or detachable form). Reasonable opportunity, e.g., 30 days. Opt-out effective until canceled in writing or electronically if agreed.
- **Exceptions** — §313.13 (service providers/joint marketing, written confidentiality contract), §313.14 (processing/administering requested transactions), §313.15 (fraud, legal compliance, subpoenas, auditors, loan purchasers). No opt-out for §14/§15; notice but no opt-out for §13.
- **Reuse/redisclosure limits** — If received under §14/§15, use only to carry out the purpose received. Outside §14/§15, may use internally but redisclose only consistent with the originating institution's privacy policy.
- **Account numbers** — Cannot be shared for marketing, even with consent. Narrow exceptions for encrypted numbers and agent/service provider marketing your own products.
- **FCRA** — GLBA notice/opt-out is in addition to FCRA disclosures. If FCRA requires affiliate-sharing disclosures, they must be included in the GLBA privacy notice.

## Enforcement

FTC, federal banking agencies, other federal regulators, and state insurance authorities enforce. FTC has jurisdiction over institutions not regulated by others and can also use FTC Act §5 for deception and unfairness.

## Key Takeaways

- GLBA Privacy Rule = notice, opt-out, and sharing limits.
- Applies to financial institutions and recipients of NPI.
- Consumer vs. customer distinction drives obligations.
- NPI is broadly defined; lists derived from NPI remain NPI.
- Three exceptions (§13, §14, §15) determine notice and opt-out requirements.
- Account numbers cannot be shared for marketing.
- Reuse and redisclosure limits follow the data.
- Enforcement is shared across FTC, banking agencies, and state authorities.
