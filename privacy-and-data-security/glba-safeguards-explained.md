# GLBA Safeguards Rule – Explanatory Note

## Purpose of the GLBA Safeguards Rule

The GLBA Safeguards Rule (16 C.F.R. Part 314) implements the security provisions of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act. Its purpose is to:

- **Protect the security and confidentiality** of customer information held by financial institutions.
- **Protect against anticipated threats or hazards** to the security or integrity of that information.
- **Protect against unauthorized access** that could result in substantial harm or inconvenience to any customer.
- **Require a written information security program (WISP)** appropriate to the institution's size, complexity, and the sensitivity of the information it handles.
- **Ensure accountability** through a designated qualified individual and annual reporting to the board.
- **Extend oversight to service providers** through contractual safeguards and periodic assessment.
- **Mandate breach notification** to the FTC for security events affecting 500+ consumers.

## Who Does it Cover- The Threshold Question: "Significantly Engaged" (See GLBA-Explained)

Applies to any business **significantly engaged** in financial activities under GLBA — the same coverage test as the Privacy Rule.
ADDITIONALLY, the Safeguards Rule also covers **"finders"** — companies that connect consumers with financial institutions for financial products.

## Core Requirements

- **WISP** — Written information security program appropriate to size and complexity.
- **Qualified individual** — A designated person (or outsourced provider) responsible for overseeing the WISP.
- **Risk assessment** — Identify reasonably foreseeable internal and external risks; assess the sufficiency of existing safeguards; implement controls to address the risks.
- **Administrative, technical, and physical safeguards** — Access controls; employee training; service provider oversight; secure disposal; monitoring and logging.
- **Multi-factor authentication (MFA)** — Required for any information system accessed by employees.
- **Encryption** — Required for customer information at rest and in transit.
- **Annual board report** — At least annually, report on the overall status of the WISP and compliance.
- **Service provider oversight** — Contractual requirements to safeguard information; periodic assessments of providers.
- **Breach notification** — Notify the FTC within **30 days** of discovering a security event affecting **500+ consumers**.
- **Evaluate and adjust** — Continuously test and update the WISP based on changes in the business or threat environment.

## Enforcement

The FTC enforces the Safeguards Rule for institutions within its jurisdiction. Violations can lead to injunctive relief and civil penalties. Federal banking agencies enforce parallel safeguards requirements for banks and other regulated entities.

## Key Distinction

Safeguards Rule = **security** of NPI. Privacy Rule = **notice, opt-out, and sharing limits**. Both apply to financial institutions, and a payments company like Airwallex is likely covered by both.

## Key Takeaways

- Safeguards Rule = security of customer information.
- WISP is mandatory and must be written.
- A qualified individual must oversee it.
- MFA and encryption are now explicit requirements.
- Annual board reporting is required.
- Breach notification: 30 days to FTC for 500+ consumers.
- Service providers must be contractually bound and periodically assessed.

## Source
